How the Sun estimate uses your location

When you choose a route, CoolRoute uses its latitude and longitude and your selected time. It first looks up forecast sunlight from Open-Meteo. If that is unavailable, it estimates the Sun’s position from the location, date, and time.

  • Latitude tells how far north or south the route is. This changes the Sun’s angle above the horizon.
  • Longitude tells how far east or west it is. Along with the time zone, it helps match clock time to the Sun’s local time.
  • Date changes the Sun’s seasonal path. Its height is usually different in summer and winter.
  • Time changes the Sun’s position during the day. Near solar noon, it is usually highest.
  • The app combines estimated sunlight with mapped tree and building shade to compare route heat exposure.

These are planning estimates, not a precise shadow or pavement-temperature measurement. Missing map features or unavailable weather data can affect accuracy.

Mathematics behind the estimates

Sun height from latitude, season, and timesin(α) = sin(φ) sin(δ) + cos(φ) cos(δ) cos(H)

α = Sun height; φ = latitude; δ = seasonal declination; H = hour angle. If α is below the horizon, sunlight is set to zero.

Season and local solar timeδ ≈ 23.45° × sin(360° × (day − 81) ÷ 365)
solar time ≈ clock time + longitude ÷ 15 − UTC offset
H = 15° × (solar time − 12)
Shade estimate100 × Σ(segment length × shade fraction) ÷ total distance

Each mapped street segment contributes according to its estimated tree/building shade.

Heat exposuresun strength × Σ[length × (1 − shade) + wait time × walking speed × (1 − crossing shade)]

More sun and less shade increase this relative score. It is a planning estimate, not a temperature.

Walking timeΣ(segment length ÷ 1.3 m/s + crossing wait)

Uses an average walking speed of 1.3 m/s, plus mapped crossing waits where available.

Live sun strengthclamp(shortwave radiation ÷ 850 W/m², 0, 1)

Open-Meteo looks up radiation for the selected coordinates and local hour. Astronomy is the backup estimate.

CoolRoute choiceroute cost = distance + λ × heat exposure

The app adjusts λ (lambda) until it finds a short route within your heat budget, when one is possible.

Shade estimates use mapped OpenStreetMap trees/buildings. A simple astronomy fallback uses the nearest 15° longitude meridian for its time-zone estimate, so it omits some orbital and clock corrections.
